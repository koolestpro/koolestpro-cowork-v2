## Goal

Build two working client acquisition engines with one shared foundation:

- **Nigeria / Africa** — the proven channel. Fast to close, referral-heavy, free-sample-driven. Near-term revenue and the base for the physical office.
- **International (US/UK/EU)** — the higher-ticket long game. Slower, proof-dependent, search-driven.

Both are being built. They share a domain, a site, a prospect pool, a CRM, and a proof system. They diverge completely at the offer, the wedge, the channel, and the price.

### The real finding

Content is Ridwan's strongest channel, not his weakest. What changed is **what the content asks for.**

| | Old content | Current content |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | TikTok build videos, YouTube/LinkedIn teaching | CRO craft carousels |
| **Implicit message** | "Watch me build a store" / "here's how to fix yours" | "Look at the quality of what I can build" |
| **Signal type** | **Service** — hire me for a job | **Employment** — hire me for a role |
| **Who responds** | Store owners with money and a problem | Recruiters, interviewers, designers |

Ridwan's own words: *"I keep attracting recruiters and getting on interviews but I have not secured a project yet."* That is the market reading the signal accurately.

The fix is not more content, better content, or a new platform. It is returning to content that asks to be hired for a **job** rather than a **role**, and giving every piece a destination that isn't a DM.

Craft mode stays — it builds authority and feeds the portfolio. It just stops being the only thing running.

## Operating Principles

1. **Fix the destination before adding traffic.** Every channel needs somewhere real to land.
2. **Price rises are earned against named proof milestones.** Never on hope.
3. **Never invent a client result.** Highest-stakes rule in the project. If there is no number, lead with scope, craft, and speed.
4. **Concept brands are always labelled as concept brands.** KURA and HALOS are portfolio work, never implied clients.
5. **Local-first holds, including the CRM.** No Notion, no external database.
6. **Reuse before building.** Most of this redirects machinery that already exists.
7. **Effort scales with interest.** Cheap artifacts wide, expensive artifacts only to repliers. Both engines obey this.
8. **One prospect pool, segmented.** A target that feeds a content post can also feed outreach. Do not fork the file.
9. **Throttle intake at 5 new projects/month.** Marketing that outruns delivery destroys the proof this plan runs on.

# Track A — Shared Foundation

## A1. Domain

Candidates, verify availability before buying:

1. `koolestpro.com`

3. `ridwanadekunle.com`

~$12/year. DNS to Vercel. **Do not buy a keyword domain** — no SEO advantage in 2026 and it signals low trust.

## A2. Site structure — one domain, two entry paths

The main site speaks to international DTC. Nigerian prospects arrive via DM and ad links, straight to their own pages. Neither audience reads copy written for the other.

```
INTERNATIONAL (main site, USD)
/                          Homepage — Shopify CRO and store builds
/services/store-build      Full build — scope, process, price
/services/page-build       Single page (PDP/LP/advertorial) — price
/services/cro-audit        Paid audit — sample report, price
/work                      Case study index
/work/{slug}               Individual case studies
/about                     Ridwan, process, location
/book                      Cal.com booking + qualifying questions
/resources/*               SEO content hub (Track F)

NIGERIA / AFRICA (₦, WhatsApp-first)
/ng                        "Get a real website for your business"
/ng/website                Packages in Naira, WhatsApp CTA
/ng/samples                Live sample sites gallery
/ng/stories                The three anchor case studies
```

**Hard rules:**
- Every service page carries a price or a range. No "contact for pricing." This single choice does more repositioning than the rest of the site.
- Nigerian pages: Naira, WhatsApp button as primary CTA, no Cal.com (booking a call is a foreign ritual to that buyer).
- International pages: USD, Cal.com booking, email CTA.
- `/ng` pages are `noindex` only if they'd confuse international SEO. Default is indexed — they'll rank locally with near-zero competition.
- Proof appears within the first screen on both.

## A3. Build method

Reuse `replo-rebuilds/portfolio/index.html` (170KB, already v2, brand-complete and responsive) as the foundation. This is a restructure into a selling site, not a from-scratch build. Host on **Vercel free tier** — same platform as the sample-site engine, so one deployment workflow serves everything.

## A4. Capture and analytics

- Cal.com free tier on `/book` — qualifying questions: store URL, monthly revenue band, timeline, budget band.
- WhatsApp click-to-chat links on all `/ng` pages, with a prefilled message so you know which page they came from.
- Plausible, Umami, or GA4. **Minimum: track `/book` submissions, WhatsApp clicks, and per-page traffic.** Without this, Track G ads can never be evaluated.

One pool, two segments. `targets_pool.json` was built to choose content subjects; it now serves outreach too. **Do not fork the file** — a target that feeds a CRO Fix post can also be a prospect, and keeping them together means content and outreach compound.

## C2. Nigeria sourcing — the ad-library method

**This is the sharpest idea in the plan.** Someone running ads is already spending money on traffic, which means they are serious about selling. If that ad's destination is a DM inbox rather than a real site, they are a qualified prospect by definition.

**Verified available** (Apify token in `.env` tested working 2026-08-02):

| Actor | Runs | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `curious_coder/facebook-ads-library-scraper` | 16.6M | Meta Ad Library — primary |
| `corner_cutter/facebook-ad-library-scraper` | 1.09M | Meta Ad Library — fallback |
| `lexis-solutions/tiktok-ads-scraper` | 167k | TikTok |
| `data_xplorer/tiktok-ads-library-fast` | 35k | TikTok — alternative |

Both Meta actors are pay-per-event. **Check the actual unit cost against the Apify console before the first real run** — it is not documented here because it was not verified, and this plan does not publish numbers it has not checked.

**Weekly sourcing routine:**

1. Query Meta Ad Library, country = Nigeria, across your target categories (fashion, beauty/perfume, kids, food, gadgets, home)
2. For each advertiser: pull page name, Instagram handle, ad creative, and **the ad's destination link**
3. Qualify mechanically:

| Ad destination | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Instagram profile / DM | **Qualified — highest priority** |
| WhatsApp link | **Qualified** |
| Linktree or similar | **Qualified** |
| Their own site, weak | Qualified — sample-site or rebuild path |
| Their own site, good | Reject |

4. Write into `targets_pool.json` with `segment: "nigeria-local"`, dedupe on Instagram handle
5. Target floor: 40+ available Nigerian prospects at all times

**Expand to Ghana and Kenya only after Nigeria is working.** Same method, same actors, different country filter.

## C3. International sourcing

Apify-based Shopify store sourcing in the $10k-200k/mo band, in categories matching the portfolio. Enrich with store URL, contact email, Instagram handle, LinkedIn company page, and a visible mobile issue. Floor: 40+ available. Weekly top-up.

## C4. Cadence

# Track D — Nigeria / Africa Engine

The proven market. Fast close, referral-heavy, free-sample-driven.

## D1. Who they are

Businesses already running Meta, Instagram, or TikTok ads. Selling through DMs. Physical shop or no shop. Either no website, or something weak. Spending real money on traffic that lands in an inbox.

**The pitch writes itself:** *"You're paying for traffic that lands in your DMs."*

## D2. The wedge ladder

Effort scales with interest, same discipline as everywhere else in this project.

| Stage | Artifact | Your cost | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Touch 1** | **Free ad creative** — one static or video ad using their real products | ~30 min | Everyone qualified |
| **Touch 2** | **Free sample site** — 5-10 products, their branding, live URL | ~60 min | Repliers only |
| **Touch 3** | Paid build conversation | Your time | Interested only |

**Why the ad creative leads:** they already buy ads, so a better ad is immediately legible value. It costs you far less than a site (`static-ad-image` skill and the TikTok video pipeline already exist). And it sets up the site perfectly, because their new ad still has nowhere good to land.

## D2a. The demo site — what you show before you build anything

A prospect cannot picture "a free sample site." They need to see one. This is the single asset that makes the whole Nigeria wedge legible, and it gets built once.

**Brand:** a fictional beauty/skincare brand — the highest-volume Instagram-selling category in Nigeria and visually rich enough to look premium. **Clearly labelled a demo.** It is never implied to be a client.

**Scope — a complete small store:**

| Page | Contents |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Hero, featured products, brand story, trust strip, footer |
| Collection | Product grid, basic filtering |
| Product page | Gallery, price in Naira, description, variants |
| Sticky cart bar | Persistent on mobile, primary action is **"Message seller on WhatsApp"** |

**Rules:**
- Naira pricing throughout, local delivery copy
- Checkout is a WhatsApp handoff, prefilled with the product name. No real cart.
- Footer credit: `Built by Koolestpro — koolestpro.com`
- Mobile-first. Most Nigerian buyers will only ever see it on a phone.

**Double duty:** this is also the **base template every real free sample is built from.** Swapping in a prospect's logo, colours, and 5-10 products is what makes the 60-minute build target realistic. Build it well once, reuse it every week.

**Where it lives:** hosted on Vercel, linked from `/ng/samples`, and sent directly in DMs as the "here's exactly what you get" link.

**Alongside it, show the real ones.** aminazaid.com and scentsavenue.ng are live and linkable. Lead with those as proof, then use the demo to set expectations for the free tier specifically — real client sites show the paid result and would over-promise on their own. (justdarlings and Jellys Glamour are down; recover screenshots before building their case studies.)

## D3. Free sample site — build spec

Target: **under 60 minutes per sample.**

1. **Pull their assets** — logo, colours, 5-10 product images from their Instagram or Facebook
2. **Choose a template** from `replo-rebuilds/templates/`:

| Prospect type | Template |
|---|---|
| Single-product seller | `pdp/` + `homepage/` |
| Multi-product retailer | `homepage/` + collection |
| Service business | `landing-page/` |
| Info/education seller | `advertorial/` or `listicle/` |

3. **Swap in** their branding, product names, Naira prices, local delivery copy
4. **Checkout is a WhatsApp or Instagram button**, not a real cart — prefilled with the product name so the DM arrives with context
5. **Footer credit:** `Built by Koolestpro — koolestpro.com`. This is the entire point.
6. **Deploy** to Vercel: `{brand-slug}.koolestpro.com` or a Vercel subdomain

**Frontend only. No backend, no admin access.** This is deliberate: it is what makes the paid upgrade necessary rather than optional.

## D4. Product import — the Excel template

The mechanism that turns a free sample into a paid conversation.

Ridwan supplies a fixed spreadsheet template. They fill it, send it back, he imports. Columns:

```
product_name | price_ngn | description | image_url_1..4 | category | in_stock | variant_options
```

Rules:
- Adding more than the free 5-10 products is **paid work**
- Images can be sent as a folder or Instagram links; no minimum quality gate, but garbage in means garbage out and that gets said upfront
- One import round is included in every paid package; further rounds are billed

This also gives you a clean, reusable onboarding artifact for every paid client.

## D5. Where the free tier ends

Free forever, no expiry, their credit-bearing site to keep and use. Paid work begins at:
- Real payment processing
- Connecting their own domain
- More than the free product count
- Any change after handover
- Backend/admin access
- Inventory, variants, shipping logic

## D6. Channel

**Instagram DM is primary** — that is where this buyer lives. WhatsApp second. Email rarely. LinkedIn never.

**Manual only.** No automation tooling on Instagram, no third-party DM software. Ban risk is real and an account ban costs you the channel permanently. The daily task (C4/E5) drafts the messages; Ridwan sends them by hand.

Volume: 15-20 qualified prospects per week.

## D7. Nigeria price ladder

| Package | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | ₦250k-500k | Template build, up to 20 products, WhatsApp checkout, one import round, basic mobile optimisation |
| **Business** | ₦500k-1M | Custom design pass, full catalogue, real payment integration, domain setup, 2 import rounds, basic SEO |
| **Premium** | ₦1M+ | Full custom build, advanced features, ongoing support window, ad creative package |

Publish these on `/ng/website`. **In a market where every quote is ad hoc and negotiated, published fixed pricing is itself the differentiator.**

## D8. Guardrails

- Max **3 free sample sites per week**, and only to prospects who replied to Touch 1
- Free ad creative capped at ~15/week
- No visible product catalogue (Instagram shop or Facebook page) → no sample
- Use their real product images. **Never generate fake product photography** — it misrepresents their goods
- Sample sites with no response after 3 touches come down after 60 days

# Track F — Inbound and Search

## F1. Restore the content that actually sold

Two formats produced all six content-sourced clients, and both are currently dormant:

- **TikTok build videos** — no voiceover, background music, watch a store get built, see the finished result. Produced Jellys Glamour and two foreign clients. **Best performer in his history.**
- **Teaching content** — practical Shopify/ecommerce guidance for store owners, on YouTube and LinkedIn. Produced the aminazaid → justdarlings → scentsavenue chain.

Craft mode (Steps 1-11 of the content plan) stays exactly as-is for authority and portfolio. It simply stops being the only thing running.

Every post in every mode now ends pointing at a service page or case study on the domain, not at engagement.

## F1a. The automated TikTok engine

**Constraint (Ridwan, 2026-08-02):** he will not commit ongoing time to recording himself building. He can supply about **3 short hook clips of himself, recorded once and reused**. Everything else must be automatic.

That constraint points at a better format than a literal screen recording. The thing that made the old videos work was watching a site *transform* — and this project already generates exactly that artifact every Saturday.

**Video structure — one human ingredient, recorded once:**

| Segment | Length | Source |
|---|---|---|

## F1b. What the TikTok data actually says (analysed 2026-08-02)

Full analysis: `docs/research/tiktok-content-analysis.md`. Raw data: `docs/research/tiktok-dekunleridwan-posts-20260802.json` (61 posts, pulled via Apify).

Five findings that should govern every video produced from here:

1. **The channel was abandoned, not exhausted.** 54 of 61 posts came in 2022-2023; seven in the two and a half years since. Median views rose every single year. Nothing declined except output.

2. **Save rate collapsed 9x while views held.** 8.3 saves per 1,000 views in 2022 → 0.9 in 2025. Saves are the buying-intent metric for a service provider: a view is attention, a save is "I will need this person later." The content kept earning attention and stopped earning intent.

3. **Posts that hand a store owner a usable thing get saved. Posts about Ridwan get nothing.** The self-introduction video ("I am a shopify website designer and I am here to give out value") earned **zero saves** at 1,144 views. An Illustrator tutorial: zero. "The money is with problem solvers": zero. Meanwhile "free images you can use on your ecommerce site" hit 24.9 saves per 1,000.

4. **Two formats won, and both are automatable:**
   - **Transformation showcase** — "Figma design made functional with Shopify page builder", 12 seconds, 11,500 views, 108 saves. Highest reach in the account *and* a strong save rate. This is exactly the F1a format.
   - **Utility post** — a tool, free resource, or specific tactic. Highest save rates in the entire dataset (14-25 per 1,000), lower reach. Cheap to produce: screen recording plus text overlays.

5. **491 followers produced three clients.** Reach is not the lever. Do not optimise for follower growth.

**Rules the data supports, applied to every video:**

- Under 20 seconds where possible (account median is 18s, best performer was 12s)
- Never make the video about Ridwan
- Never aim at designers — that audience saves nothing
- Every post carries a usable artifact: a tool, a resource, a before/after, a tactic
- No abstract or motivational statements without an artifact attached
- **Track save rate as the KPI, not views**

**Also:** the TikTok bio currently points at email. Repoint it at the domain once Track A ships.

**Add the utility post as a second automated stream.** F1a covers the transformation format. The utility format is higher-intent per view and cheaper to make, and nothing in the current pipeline produces it. One of each per week is a realistic restart.

**Built:** `store-templates/02-skincare-beauty/` — Lumora Skin, 12 products, three pages, 1.6MB. Cream and sand base, terracotta clay accent, Fraunces over Inter. Section names follow the vertical rather than the engine's watch origins (The Formula, The Routine, The Kitchen, not The Calibre / Anatomy / The Atelier). Deployed to `site/public/live/lumora/`, so the koolestpro.com sample-site link is unchanged.

**The engine is now a backwards-compatible superset, not a fork.** Template 01's config runs on it unchanged, because every addition falls back to Template 01's own wording and ids. Three generalisations and two bug fixes:

- **`labels` block** — Template 01 hard-coded "Add to selection", "owners", "pieces", four watch-specific PDP assurances and "Worn with, or instead of." into the engine itself. All of it is config now. This is what stops a reskin for a new vertical from reading like a watch shop, and it is the single biggest thing that made this a 30-minute reskin rather than a rewrite.

Template 01 hard-coded `#0A0A0B` as text-on-accent in six places, which is invisible on a cream background. The stylesheet now hard-codes no colour at all, so the same sheet renders a dark theme if a config asks for one. A light theme also has to build depth with shadow where a dark one gets it free from contrast, so this was real work, not a token swap.
- **Bug: review-rail thumbs were `loading="lazy"`** but the rail sits up to 3,400px off-screen horizontally, so they only fetched once the marquee carried them in and popped in blank. Ten 44px images cost less than that. (Correcting an earlier claim in this entry: this one was self-inflicted, introduced while writing the new engine and caught by QA the same day. Template 01 never had the attribute.)
- **Bug: `#toast` hid itself with `translateY(140%)`**, which on a short message cleared the viewport by only 17px and rendered as a stray dark pill in full-page screenshots — which matters, because this project makes marketing mockups out of full-page captures. Now `calc(100% + 60px)` plus an opacity fade. **This one is real in Template 01, and was back-ported the same day (see below).**

**Imagery:** 16 gpt-image-2 renders at `--quality low`, straight to WebP, 1.5MB total, all sharing one background and lighting sentence so the grid reads as a single shoot. Three needed a re-shoot after review: the hero subject had landed under the headline (the desktop scrim is weighted left, so the subject has to sit in the right 40%), the promo box was too small and in the wrong half (that scrim is weighted the other way), and the eye cream read as a duplicate of the clay mask. Product labels are left deliberately blank — an invented brand name printed on every bottle reads worse and makes the set unusable for a reskin.

**`qa.py` — the template now ships its own verification harness**, and this is the part most worth reusing. It drives real Chromium against all three pages at 1440×900 and 390×844 and checks 117 things: console and page errors, failed first-party requests, preloader teardown, every image decoded *after a full scroll*, horizontal overflow at both widths, every `data-reveal` actually revealed, the chrome on each page, grid and panel counts, stat counters, FAQ accordion, filter counts, sort order, quantity, variants, bag add/decrement/subtotal/close, mobile menu, and the sticky filter offset. Final: **117 passed, 0 failed**, run against both the template source and the deployed copy.

Three things learned building it that apply to any future harness here:

1. **A naive "are all images loaded" check fails on its own laziness.** The first run reported 7 failures that were all artifacts of checking the first fold without scrolling. Walking the page top to bottom first turns a false alarm into a genuinely stronger test, and it is what surfaced the real review-rail bug underneath.
2. **Never wait on `load`.** GSAP, Lenis and Google Fonts are three third parties that can each hang it. Google Fonts and cdnjs both went unreachable from the sandbox mid-session and killed two runs. The harness now waits on a DOM signal and takes a `--vendor-dir` that serves the libraries and real font faces from local files, which makes QA deterministic and also puts the correct typography into the screenshots.
3. **Assertions are not enough — look at the output.** Both of the genuinely user-visible defects in this build passed all 117 checks and were only caught by eye: the mobile hero was scrimmed to near-invisible so it read as a plain cream text block with no photograph, and the toast pill showed up in full-page captures. Fixed by giving mobile its own scrim (photo keeps the top of the frame, cream rises behind the copy) and its own `object-position`, since a portrait crop of a landscape hero otherwise cuts the subject out entirely.

**Also changed:** the corner watermark is hidden below 640px — it is a fixed badge, so on a narrow screen it landed on whatever sat bottom-left, which on the PDP was the product title.
