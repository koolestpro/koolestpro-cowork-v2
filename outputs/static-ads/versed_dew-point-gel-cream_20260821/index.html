The four ads

01 · Problem / Solution

Names the annoyance before the product appears. Pain-point headline with the word "greasy" struck through, product small in the corner as the answer.

Source: "never feels greasy", "won't clog pores", "for normal to oily skin"

02 · Benefit / Result

Clean hero shot with four benefit chips wired to the jar by leader lines. The most "product photography" of the set.

Source: absorbs instantly, never greasy, sits under makeup, won't clog pores, 2 fl oz / 60 ml

03 · Social Proof / Trust

A real buyer's line carries the credibility instead of the brand's own claim. Rating and review count sit above the quote.

Source: 4.7/5, 456 reviews, Graziella C. quote, PDP trust badges

04 · Offer / Urgency

Cart-retargeting frame rather than a fake countdown. There is no live Versed promo to reference, so the urgency comes from the cart, not an invented deadline.

Source: $14.99 list price, Vegan / Dermatologist-Tested / Cruelty-Free badges

Most product ads fail for a boring reason: one image is asked to do four jobs at once.

I took Versed's Dew Point gel-cream moisturizer, $14.99, 4.7 stars across 456 reviews, and built four separate ad directions off the same jar.

Problem/solution. The headline names the annoyance before the product turns up. Moisturizer that leaves oily skin shiny by noon. The jar sits small in the corner as the answer, not the hero.

Benefit/result. Clean hero shot, four callouts wired to the jar with leader lines. Absorbs instantly. Never greasy. Sits under makeup. Won't clog pores. Every one of those lines came off Versed's own product page.

Social proof. A real buyer's line does the work: "It's like a fresh glass of water for thirsty skin." Rating and review count sit right above it.

Offer/urgency. Retargeting frame. Still thinking about it, your cart is waiting, one CTA bar loud enough that you can't scroll past it.

Same jar. Four different reasons to stop.

One creative is not a test. It's just the first thing you thought of.
