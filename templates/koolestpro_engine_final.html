03  Post Copy
LinkedIn · Long-form
You build the page first. Then guess why it doesn't convert. I've done this. Most Shopify developers have. You open your builder, start building, make layout decisions on the fly, show the client a half-built page, get vague feedback, rebuild it twice, and still wonder why the final result feels off. The fix isn't a better tool. It's a better order. Design First, Develop Fast: → Open Figma before you touch your builder. Always. → Wireframe first — blocks and text only. Solve structure before style. → High-fidelity second — apply brand, hierarchy, spacing. Get client approval on this. → Lock the design. Not "good enough to start" — actually locked. → Then open Shopify Liquid, Replo, or Instant and execute. Every decision is already made. The result: Build time drops from 8 hours to 2.5. Revision rounds go from 4 to 1. Clients pay more because the process looks premium. If you're making a design decision inside the builder, you skipped a step. Save this. Share it with a Shopify developer who's still building blind.
LinkedIn · Hook Post
Most Shopify developers are designers inside the builder. That's the problem. Your builder is for executing. Figma is for deciding. When those two jobs get mixed up, you rebuild the same section 4 times and still don't know why the page feels wrong. Lock the design first. Then build. What does your current workflow look like — Figma first, or straight to the builder?
