# Reference research — sites in the register koolestpro.com is aiming at

**Date:** 2026-08-23
**Method:** visited each site in a real browser, read its actual copy and looked
at its layout. Not summarised from listicles. Two sites could not be assessed
(noted below) and are excluded rather than guessed at.
**Purpose:** replace guesswork in the koolestpro.com design direction with
observed patterns.

## The five findings that actually change the build

### 1. Studios that sell visual work lead with the work, not with an abstraction

Exo Ape's first viewport is a photograph. No headline at all — just the logo, a
four-item nav, and "Scroll to explore". Basement runs a headline over dark
footage. Neither opens on an abstract graphic.

koolestpro.com opened on a canvas particle field. It looked good and said
nothing about what Ridwan does. **Changed:** the hero now plays a real scroll
capture of a real build, offset right so the display type keeps clean space.

### 2. "Best experienced in motion" is a section, and Ridwan already had the assets

Exo Ape devotes a homepage section to a reel: *"Our work is best experienced in
motion. Don't forget to put on your headphones."*

`outputs/templates/` already holds bezel-framed scroll videos for HALOS, KURA,
Champion Biotics, Tap and Rate, Jellys Glamour and Captain & Skipper — most
already web-sized. The site was using none of them. **Changed:** an "In motion"
section now plays two real captures, and the hero uses a third.

### 3. The whole competitor category sells on numbers Ridwan does not have

Fuel Made's homepage is built from metrics: a 2.5% → 4.1% animation, then four
figures per case study (+59% desktop conversion, +118% placed-order rate, +87%
conversions, −35% bounce). Every case study repeats the pattern.

koolestpro.com cannot compete on that axis today, and inventing figures is the
one thing the growth plan forbids outright. But there are two workable answers,
both observed:

- **Basement writes narrative outcomes instead**: *"sold out inventory in
  hours"*, *"a storefront built for millions"*. Concrete, memorable, no
  percentage.
- **Designjoy publishes no client metrics at all.** It substitutes risk reversal
  and transparency (see 5).

**Changed:** the no-metric case-study state no longer opens with an apology. An
ongoing engagement now reads as the result — *"Still building for them today.
The engagement outlasted the project it started as"* — with the missing
percentage noted plainly underneath rather than leading.

### 4. Published pricing is genuinely rare, and Ridwan's is below market

Neither Fuel Made nor any agency reviewed publishes a price. Designjoy makes its
$4,995/month the loudest element on the page and converts well on it.

Separately: US freelance Shopify builds commonly run **$2,000–5,000**
([Folio3](https://ecommerce.folio3.com/blog/shopify-website-design-pricing/),
[OuterBox](https://www.outerboxdesign.com/articles/web-design/shopify-website-design-pricing/)).
koolestpro's full store build is published at **$1,200–1,800** — under the floor
of the range his international targets already expect to pay. Pricing below
market does not read as value to that buyer; it reads as a different tier of
service.

**Not changed — this is Ridwan's call.** The growth plan gates price rises on
named proof milestones, so raising them unilaterally would break his own rule.
Flagged for decision.

### 5. Solo is a selling point when you name it

Designjoy states it outright: *"Designjoy is run entirely by Brett. Designjoy
doesn't hire extra designers or outsource work."* It explains the price, the
speed and exactly who you get.

koolestpro.com never said Ridwan works alone. **Changed:** the hero now reads
"One person, not an agency."

## Secondary observations, not yet acted on

- **Risk reversal replaces metrics as the trust mechanism.** Designjoy: pause or
  cancel anytime, and 75% back inside a week. koolestpro.com has none. This is a
  commercial offer, not a design decision, so it needs Ridwan to set the terms
  before anything goes on the page.
- **One committed concept beats a good palette.** Darkroom is literally a
  darkroom: black, red safelight, monospace. koolestpro.com has a strong look but
  not yet an idea underneath it.
- **Small interactive switches recur.** by-kin has a layout toggle (1/2);
  Basement has a HUMAN/MACHINE switch. Cheap, memorable, and they make a visitor
  a participant rather than a reader.
- **Even the 404 stays in concept.** Basement's is a security-camera feed with a
  running timecode.
- **Copy volume is genuinely low at this tier.** Exo Ape's homepage is roughly 90
  words, by-kin's is one sentence. koolestpro.com at ~120 words is in range, so
  the density direction was correct — this research validates it rather than
  changing it.
- **Award-winning does not mean heavy.** Obys could not be assessed because its
  preloader never finished. koolestpro.com renders in ~300kB with no preloader,
  which is worth more than an award for the Nigerian half of the audience.

---

## Decisions for Ridwan

1. **Pricing.** Raise international prices toward the $2,000–5,000 band, or hold
   until the proof milestones in the growth plan are met? Currently below market.
2. **Risk reversal.** Is there an offer he is willing to stand behind — a
   revision guarantee, a paid-audit-credited-against-build, a first-week refund?
3. **Testimonials.** Still the single biggest unlock. Four messages.
