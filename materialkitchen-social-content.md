## Post — 2026-07-19 · Homepage above-the-fold CRO Fix

### CRO-MATERIALKITCHEN-CAROUSEL (LinkedIn / Instagram)

```
Material Kitchen makes some of the best-looking kitchen gear I've come across.

And their homepage still leaves sales on the table.

Here's what you get when you land on it on mobile: a beautiful hero photo, a headline, and one button that says "Shop The MK Free Collection."

That's the whole above-the-fold. No price. No rating. No proof. The "free shipping, 30-day trial, guaranteed for life" part is real — but it's stuck in a tiny bar at the very top that nobody reads. And on a fresh visit, a full-screen email popup can drop over all of it before you've seen anything.

So a first-time buyer is quietly asking "is this actually any good?" and the page hands them nothing to hold onto until they've scrolled past about 12,000 pixels of it.

I rebuilt the above-the-fold. Same brand, same photo, same fonts. All I did was move the selling to where the eye already is:

- Killed the popup so the hero shows straight away
- Put a real 5-star customer quote right under the headline
- Pulled the guarantee, trial and shipping into visible trust chips
- Surfaced a real bestseller with its real price ($195, down from $225)
- Made the button carry the offer instead of a vague "shop"

None of this is a redesign. The store was already gorgeous. The fix was clearing the path and putting proof, price and trust in front of people before they go looking for it.

If your store looks great but the sales don't match, this is usually the reason.

— Ridwan
```

Material Kitchen's Trio of Knives sits at a 5.0 rating from 472 reviews. I wanted to see how many honest angles I could pull out of that one product page.

Four ad directions, same three knives, nothing invented.

Problem: most people own a bulky 12-piece knife set and only ever reach for two blades in it. The Trio replaces the whole block with three full-tang, Japanese-steel knives.

Benefit: full-tang balance, a 13-degree edge, cryogenic tempering, and that 472-review rating. All of it is on their own product page, I didn't add anything.

Social proof: I could have written a testimonial that sounded better. Instead I used a real one, from a verified buyer named Mara T.: "Sleek design, perfect weight, and very sharp. I love them."

Offer: the set is actually $30 off right now, $225 down to $195, so the urgency ad leans on a real discount instead of a countdown timer that resets every week.

Four ads, one product, four different reasons to buy it depending on what the person scrolling past already believes.

The job here was picking which true fact to lead with for each kind of shopper.

This is concept work. I have no relationship with Material Kitchen, I just liked the knives enough to think through how I'd sell them.

Ridwan
