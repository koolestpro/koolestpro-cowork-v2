Ever land on a product page and get hit with two pop-ups before you even see what it costs?

That's what happens on this NFC review-card store. Cookie consent modal first. Dismiss it, an email quiz pops up asking which "card is actually you." Dismiss that, and the cookie modal comes back.

You still haven't seen the price.

Here's what I changed and why:

1. Removed the blocking popup sequence — page loads straight to the product
2. Added a visible single-unit price under the title (the only pricing on the live page is a bulk table, buried past the Add to Cart button)
3. Moved the benefit bullets above the style picker — right now you're asked to choose before you're told why it matters
4. Repositioned the chat bubble off the sticky CTA bar (it was overlapping "Add to Cart")
5. Swapped the hero image for the clean studio shot — the current one has a SumUp terminal in the background
6. Added a shipping/returns line near the CTA
7. Paired the 5-star rating with a supporting trust badge (it's currently backed by 2 reviews)

Same product, same brand. Just put in the order a buyer actually needs it.

High-converting PDPs answer "why" before they ask "which one." This one does it backwards.

Look at the order this page puts you through: pick a style, pick a quantity, then — eventually — find out what you're buying is actually good for. The benefits are real (tap to leave a review, works with Google/TripAdvisor/Facebook, builds your online reputation) but they show up after the decision, not before it.

What great pages do differently:

1. Price sits right under the title — no guessing, no scrolling to find it
2. Benefits come before configuration — you know why it matters before you're asked to choose
3. Trust signals get reinforced, not left thin — a 5-star badge next to "2 reviews" needs backup, not just placement
4. Nothing blocks the first view — no stacked pop-ups between arrival and the product
5. Shipping and returns sit near the button, not buried in policy pages

The brand doesn't need a redesign. The information just needs to show up in the order a buyer's brain actually wants it.
