5. Swapped the hero image for a clean shot with no competing brand in frame
6. Added a return/guarantee line near the CTA
7. Paired the 5-star rating with a supporting trust badge, since it's currently backed by 2 reviews

Same product, same brand, same price. Just put the reasons to buy before the decisions to make.

High-converting PDPs answer "why" before they ask "which one." This one asks backwards, even though everything it needs is already on the page.

Look at the order this page puts you through: pick a style, pick a quantity, then eventually reach the price and the reasons the product is worth it. The benefits are real (tap to leave a review, works with Google, TripAdvisor and Facebook, builds your online reputation) and the price is right there too. They're just placed after the decision, not before it.

What great pages do differently:

1. Price and the strongest benefit sit right under the title, no scrolling required
2. The full benefit list and bulk pricing stay together lower down, for when the buyer's ready to compare quantities
3. Trust signals get reinforced, not left thin (a 5-star badge next to "2 reviews" needs backup, not just placement)
4. Nothing blocks the first view. No stacked pop-ups between arrival and the product
5. A return or guarantee line sits near the button, closing the last bit of doubt

The brand doesn't need a redesign. The information just needs to show up in the order a buyer's brain actually wants it.
