CRO FINDINGS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FINDING 1
Area: Above the Fold
Problem: A tall black promotional banner ("NFC Google Review Stands & Cards — Get More Google Reviews Fast" / "Ships Worldwide" / "GET STARTED →") occupies the entire top ~230px of the 390×844 mobile viewport, pushing the actual brand logo, hamburger menu, and cart icon below it.
Why it hurts: The banner restates the page title in generic terms instead of leading with a concrete offer, number, or proof point. It also delays brand recognition — a first-time visitor scrolls past a text block before ever seeing the TAPro logo.
Fix: Collapse the banner to a single thin strip (shipping/urgency line only, ~40-48px) or remove it, and let the logo/nav row sit at the true top of the page.
Content angle: Angle 1 (numbered before/after)
Priority: High

FINDING 2
Area: Visual Hierarchy
Problem: There are two separate search icons stacked in the same top-right corner: a "custom-mobile-header-search-icon" sits at roughly x:314-338, y:25-49 — directly on top of the promo banner's headline text (overlapping the word "Get" in "Get More Google Reviews Fast") — while the real header search icon ("top-search-toggle") sits correctly further down at y:172-216, next to the logo row.
Why it hurts: A search icon rendered mid-sentence over live headline copy reads as a rendering bug, not a design choice — it undermines trust before the visitor reads a single product detail, and it's redundant with the working search icon 150px below it.
Fix: Remove or hide the duplicate "custom-mobile-header-search-icon" instance in the banner section; keep only the header-row search icon.
Content angle: Angle 1 (numbered before/after)
Priority: Critical

FINDING 3
Area: Value Proposition / Trust Signals
Problem: Within the entire first mobile screen (844px tall, before any scrolling), there is no product name, no price, no star rating or review count, and no CTA tied to a specific product — just the banner described above and a large lifestyle photo of an NFC review stand that continues past the fold.
Why it hurts: A first-time visitor has to scroll blind, with no early signal of what a card/stand actually costs or how well-reviewed the product itself is, before deciding whether to keep going.
Fix: Add a compact headline + price range + star rating strip directly under the logo row, above or beside the hero photo, so the core offer is legible in the first screen.
Content angle: Angle 2 (buyer psychology)
Priority: High

FINDING 4
Area: Trust Signals & Social Proof
Problem: The page's strongest trust asset — "Trusted by over 15,500 U.S. businesses," with real recognizable logos (Sheraton, Panda Express, McDonald's, Home Depot, Toyota, Mercedes) — only appears after scrolling roughly 800px down, well past a short explainer video section.
Why it hurts: This is exactly the kind of proof that overcomes "is this a legitimate product" hesitation, and it's currently invisible to anyone who doesn't scroll past the first two sections.
Fix: Pull a condensed version of the trust strip (the "15,500 U.S. businesses" line plus 3-4 logos) up into the above-the-fold zone, near the hero image or CTA.
Content angle: Angle 3 (decision guide)
Priority: Medium

FINDING 5
Area: CTA Effectiveness
Problem: The primary "GET STARTED →" button in the banner links to a broad catalog page (`/collections/all-products-google-review-tools-hub`) that renders roughly 360 product cards, rather than to a specific flagship product or a short guided picker.
Why it hurts: A visitor who clicks "Get Started" expecting a next step is instead dropped into an unfiltered 360-item wall of near-identical NFC cards/stands, with no guidance on which one fits their business — high-intent traffic loses momentum right at the moment it converts.
Fix: Point the primary CTA at the single best-selling product page (or a 3-option "pick your format" mini-picker), not the full unfiltered collection hub.
Content angle: Angle 2 (buyer psychology)
Priority: High
