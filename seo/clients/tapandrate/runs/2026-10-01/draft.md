# How to Get More Google Reviews: A Practical Guide for UK Businesses

Most of your customers leave happy. Very few of them leave a review. That gap is where your Google ranking is won or lost.

If you want to know how to get more Google reviews, the answer is simpler than most guides make it. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest, cut every step between "sure" and the five stars, and do it every single day. This guide shows you how to do that for your type of business, and how to stay on the right side of Google's rules while you do it.

## Why don't happy customers leave Google reviews?

Because nobody asked at the right time, and leaving one takes too many steps. Happy customers mean to do it. Then they get home, the moment passes, and your listing never hears about it.

Think about what a review asks of someone. They have to open Google, search for your business, find the right listing, scroll to the reviews, tap "Write a review", maybe sign in, then type something. That's five or six steps for a favour. Most people give up after the second.

Unhappy customers push through those steps. Happy ones usually don't. That's why so many good local businesses sit on a lower rating than they deserve.

## How to get more Google reviews: the short version

Here's the whole method in five steps:

1. Get your direct Google review link, so customers land straight on the review box.
2. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest, not days later.
3. Make it one tap or one scan, not a search.
4. Get your team asking every customer, in their own words, with no pressure.
5. Reply to every review, good and bad.

The rest of this guide covers each step in practice.

## Step 1: Get your Google review link

Your review link opens the "write a review" box for your business directly. No searching, no wrong listing.

To find it, open your Google Business Profile (search your business name on Google while signed in, or use the Google Business Profile app), then choose **Ask for reviews** or **Share review form**. Google gives you a short link you can copy.

Use that link everywhere:

- Receipts and invoices
- Booking confirmation and follow-up emails
- Your WhatsApp replies after a job
- Your website footer and contact page

## Step 2: Ask at the moment customers are happiest

The best time to ask for a review is the moment your customer says something nice. That's when they're most willing, and it takes them seconds. The right moment looks different in every business.

### Restaurants, cafés and pubs: when the bill arrives

The meal is done, the service was good, and they're sitting with their phone out to pay. A [table coin](/collections/medium-coins) by the card machine or on the table invites them to tap while they wait.

### Salons, barbers and beauty: at the mirror or the front desk

The best moment in a salon is when the client sees the finished look. A [review stand](/collections/nfc-stands) on the reception desk turns "I love it" into a review before they've put their coat on.

### Shops: at the till

Customers already have their phones in hand at the till. A stand next to the card reader, or a [window sticker](/collections/nfc-window-stickers) on the door, catches them at the right time.

### Trades and mobile services: at sign-off

There's no counter in a customer's kitchen. Ask when you hand over the finished job, and let them tap a [Google review card](/collections/nfc-cards) or a [phone tag](/collections/phone-tags-nfc-qr) on the back of your own phone.

### Car dealers and garages: at handover

Handing over keys is the happiest moment in the whole process. Have a card ready on the desk so the review takes seconds, not a follow-up email they'll ignore.

## Step 3: Make it one tap, not five

The fewer steps between "yes" and the review box, the more reviews you get. A tap or a scan beats any link a customer has to type or search for.

| QR code | Opens the camera and scans | In person, and older phones |

## Step 4: Train your team to ask, without pressure

A tap point only works if someone points it out. The difference between a stand that gets used and one that collects dust is one sentence from your team.

Keep it short, natural and optional. A few scripts that work:

- "Glad you enjoyed it. If you've got a second, tap here and leave us a quick Google review. It really helps us."
- "If you're happy with the job, there's a review card here. You can tap it now or scan it later, whatever's easier."
- "We're a small business, so reviews make a big difference. No pressure at all, but the stand's right there."

Ask everyone, not just the customers you think are happy. And don't set staff targets for a number of reviews. More on why below.

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Replying shows future customers that you listen, and it encourages more people to leave reviews. Thank the good ones by name and answer the bad ones calmly and helpfully.

A short, specific reply beats a template. "Thanks Sarah, glad the colour came out how you wanted it" reads far better than "Thank you for your feedback!".

## What not to do: Google's rules on asking for reviews

You're allowed to ask every customer for a review. You're not allowed to bribe them, filter them or pressure them. Break these rules and Google can remove your reviews or restrict your profile.

Google's own guidance is clear on these points:

- **No incentives.** Google says offering "free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews" is "strictly prohibited" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). No discounts, freebies or prize draws for reviews.
- **No review gating.** You can't "selectively solicit positive reviews". Ask every customer, not just the happy ones ([Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)).
- **No pressure in person.** Merchants "should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises". An invitation to tap is fine. Standing over someone until they do it is not.
- **No staff quotas.** Google lists asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that name a staff member, as rating manipulation.
- **Never buy reviews.** Fake reviews get removed, and they can take your real ones down with them.

The good news is that the right way is also the easiest way. Make reviewing quick and optional for everyone, and the reviews follow.

There's no magic number, but more recent reviews and a higher average both help you stand out in local results. What matters most is a steady flow of new reviews, not a one-off burst.

## FAQ

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?
No. Google prohibits offering discounts, free products or any other reward in exchange for a review. You can ask, but you can't pay.

### Is it OK to ask customers for a review in person?
Yes. Asking in person is fine as long as you ask everyone and don't pressure anyone. Let customers tap now or later, whichever suits them.

### Do NFC review cards work on iPhone?
Yes. Most iPhones from the XS onwards read NFC without an app. Our Google review stands, cards and table coins also carry a QR code for older phones.

### How do I find my Google review link?
Open your Google Business Profile while signed in and choose "Ask for reviews" or "Share review form". Google gives you a short link to copy and share.
