# Bala homepage hero: which variant and why (2026-10-11)

Board: `board.png`. Each variant is a family from `../README.md`, built on real Bala assets.

**Chosen: V1, range flatlay + product labels (Serenova / Tofu Go family).**

- It shows the whole range in one picture: their own Studio to Street Kit flatlay puts the
  weights (Bangles) next to the activewear (Bala Bra, Flared Legging, Everyday Sweatshirt) in
  one colorway. A first-time visitor sees "weights + clothes, matching, cute" before reading a
  word. This is the fix for the narrowing problem in the first two passes.
- The labels on the image name the products, so the text stack under it stays short and
  centered: proof line, 2-line H1, one-line sub, one CTA, category pills, press strip.
- Pastel flatlay on blush is Bala's own visual language (their kit and PDP renders), so it
  reads as their brand, not a template.

**Runner-up: V3, full-bleed photo with scrim (Majco family).** The most emotional of the four
and the closest to their current video hero, but the products are small in the frame (ankle
and wrist Bangles only) and the range has to be carried by pills. Good A/B challenger.

**Not chosen:**
- V2, lifestyle top: the strongest "make it cute" photo on their site, but the Bangles in it
  are the Bala x Pucci collab, which is sold out. A hero shouldn't feature a product you
  can't buy.
- V4, text first: clear, but the first thing on screen is text again, which is what Ridwan
  flagged; the image band lands below the CTA.

Copy used across all four (real facts only): H1 "Weights & activewear, but make it cute."
(their own line, now saying what they sell), sub "Wearable weights, Pilates props and matching
sets.", proof "4.9 from 1,865 Bala Bangles reviews" (labelled as Bangles reviews because that
is what the number covers), CTA "Shop best sellers", press = their own "As seen on" logos (The
Wall Street Journal, goop, Glamour).
