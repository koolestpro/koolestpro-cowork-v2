# How to Get More Google Reviews: A Practical Guide for UK Businesses

Most of your customers leave happy. Very few of them leave a review. And when someone nearby searches for what you do, those missing reviews are part of what they compare you on.

If you want to know how to get more Google reviews, the answer is simpler than most guides make it. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest, cut out the steps between "sure" and the review box, and make asking part of every day. Below is how that looks in a café, a salon, a shop, a gym, a van and a showroom, plus the Google rules you need to stay inside.

## Why don't happy customers leave Google reviews?

Because nobody asked at the right time, and leaving one takes too many steps. Happy customers mean to do it. Then they get home, the moment passes, and your listing never hears about it.

Think about what a review asks of someone. They have to open Google, search for your business, find the right listing, scroll to the reviews, tap "Write a review", maybe sign in, then type something. That's five or six steps for a favour, and every one of them is a chance to put the phone away.

Annoyed customers tend to push through those steps anyway. Happy ones often don't, which is how a good local business ends up with a rating that undersells it.

## How to get more Google reviews: the short version

Five steps:

1. Get your direct Google review link, so customers land straight on the review box.
2. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest, not days later.
3. Make it one tap or one scan, not a search.
4. Get your team asking every customer, in their own words, with no pressure.
5. Reply to every review, good and bad.

## Step 1: Get your Google review link

Your review link opens the "write a review" box for your business directly, so customers don't have to search for you or guess which listing is yours.

To find it, open your Google Business Profile (search your business name on Google while signed in, or use the Google Business Profile app), then choose **Ask for reviews** or **Share review form**. Google gives you a short link you can copy.

Use that link everywhere:

- Receipts and invoices
- Booking confirmation and follow-up emails
- Your WhatsApp replies after a job
- Your website footer and contact page

## Step 2: Ask at the moment customers are happiest

Ask when the customer says something nice. That's when they're most willing, and it only takes them a few seconds. Where that moment falls depends on the business.

### Restaurants, cafés and pubs: when the bill arrives

The meal is done, the service was good, and they're sitting with their phone out to pay. A [table coin](/collections/medium-coins) by the card machine or on the table invites them to tap while they wait.

### Salons, barbers and beauty: at the mirror or the front desk

The best moment in a salon is when the client sees the finished look. A [review stand](/collections/nfc-stands) on the reception desk turns "I love it" into a review before they've put their coat on.

### Shops: at the till

Customers usually have their phone in hand at the till anyway. A stand next to the card reader, or a [window sticker](/collections/nfc-window-stickers) on the door, catches them at the right time.

### Trades and mobile services: at sign-off

There's no counter in a customer's kitchen. Ask when you hand over the finished job, and let them tap a [Google review card](/collections/nfc-cards) or a [phone tag](/collections/phone-tags-nfc-qr) on the back of your own phone.

### Gyms and fitness studios: after a class or a milestone

Members are happiest straight after a good class, a new personal best or their first month. A stand on the front desk or a [tile](/collections/nfc-tiles-plates) by the exit catches them on the way out, and a review link in your "well done" message reaches the ones who rushed off.

### Car dealers and garages: at handover

Handing over the keys is the best moment in the whole sale. Have a card ready on the desk so the review happens there, rather than relying on a follow-up email that gets ignored.

## Step 3: Make it one tap, not five

Fewer steps means more finished reviews. A tap or a scan beats any link a customer has to type in or search for.

## Step 4: Train your team to ask, without pressure

A tap point only works if someone mentions it. Usually that's one sentence from whoever is serving the customer.

Keep it short, natural and optional. A few scripts that work:

- "Glad you enjoyed it. If you've got a second, tap here and leave us a quick Google review. It really helps us."
- "If you're happy with the job, there's a review card here. You can tap it now or scan it later, whatever's easier."
- "We're a small business, so reviews make a big difference. No pressure at all, but the stand's right there."

Placement matters as much as the script. Put the stand where people pause (the till, the reception desk, the table while they wait for the card machine) and keep it in clear view rather than behind a till screen or a pile of menus.

Ask everyone, not just the customers you think are happy. And don't set staff targets for a number of reviews. More on why below.

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Replies show future customers that someone is listening, and Google recommends responding as part of getting more reviews. Thank the good ones by name and answer the bad ones calmly.

A short, specific reply beats a template. "Thanks Sarah, glad the colour came out how you wanted it" reads far better than "Thank you for your feedback!".

## What not to do: Google's rules on asking for reviews

You can ask every customer for a review. You can't reward them for it, pick only the happy ones, or pressure anyone. Google treats those as rating manipulation and can remove reviews or restrict your profile.

What Google's own pages say:

- Offering "free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews" is "strictly prohibited" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)). So no discounts, freebies or prize draws for reviews.
- Merchants can't "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers" ([Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). Ask everyone, including the customer who seemed a bit quiet.
- Merchants "should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises". Pointing out a stand is fine. Waiting by the table until they've done it is not.
- Asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that mention a staff member by name, is also on Google's list.
- Bought or fake reviews break the same policy and get removed.

Handily, the version Google allows is also the one customers prefer: quick, optional, and offered to everyone.

## How many Google reviews do you need?

There isn't a set number. Google says good reviews can improve your visibility, and customers notice when the newest review is from last year, so a steady trickle beats one big push.

## FAQ

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?
No. Google prohibits offering discounts, free products or any other reward in exchange for a review. You can ask, but you can't pay.

### Is it OK to ask customers for a review in person?
Yes. Asking in person is fine as long as you ask everyone and don't pressure anyone. Let customers tap now or later, whichever suits them.

### Do NFC review cards work on iPhone?
Yes. Most iPhones from the XS onwards read NFC without an app. Our Google review stands, cards and table coins also carry a QR code for older phones.

### How do I find my Google review link?
Open your Google Business Profile while signed in and choose "Ask for reviews" or "Share review form". Google gives you a short link to copy and share.
