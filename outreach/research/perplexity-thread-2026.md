# Is it possible to automate my cold outreach? What way would you recommend I do it? My automated inbound method was 100% free. Yes everything was free. I use Make.com and the free plan was enough for me. Even if cold outreach would cost my money, what is the easiest cheapest way to go about it? Yes, you can absolutely automate cold outreach end‑to‑end in 2026, and you can do it very cheaply (near‑free at small scale, or ~\$30–\$80/month for a serious setup). Below is the most cost‑effective, advanced multi‑stage system I recommend for your skills and comfort with complex automation.

## The cheapest robust architecture (Make + self‑hosted n8n)

### Core idea

- Use **Make.com (free)** for light orchestration and Google Sheets as your “CRM.”
- Use a **self‑hosted n8n instance** (free software, ~\$5–\$10/month VPS) for heavy lifting: scraping, enrichment, multi‑step sequences, and conditional logic.[^2_1][^2_2]
- Send via **Gmail/Google Workspace** on secondary domains with proper DNS (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) to protect your main domain.[^2_3][^2_4][^2_5]

This gives you a powerful, multi‑stage pipeline at minimal cost.

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 1 – Lead sourcing (free/very cheap)

**Goal:** Build a targeted list of e‑commerce brands that need your services.

**Free/cheap methods:**

- **Google Maps + manual search**: “Shopify store Lagos,” “DTC brand Nigeria,” “ecommerce brand [niche].”
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator free trial** → export companies hiring “Head of Marketing,” “E‑commerce Manager.”
- **BuiltWith / Wappalyzer free**: Find sites using Shopify, then filter by traffic/niche.
- **X/Twitter search**: “looking for Shopify developer,” “need CRO expert.”

**Automation:**

- In n8n, use HTTP Request + HTML Extract nodes to scrape Google Maps or niche directories into a Google Sheet (columns: brand, URL, niche, location, signal, contact_name, email, status).[^2_2]

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 2 – Enrichment \& email finding (mostly free)

**Goal:** Get verified emails for decision‑makers.

**Free tools to stack:**

- **Apollo free plan** (limited credits/month)
- **Hunter free tier** (50 searches/month)
- **Cleanlist** (25 verifications/day, no signup)[^2_6]
- **MyEmailVerifier** (100/day) or **Verifalia** (25/day) for ongoing free verification.[^2_7][^2_8]

**Automation flow (n8n):**

1. For each new lead row, call Apollo/Hunter APIs to find emails.
2. Pass emails through Cleanlist/MyEmailVerifier API or upload in batches daily.
3. Write back to Google Sheets: `email`, `email_status` (valid/risky/invalid).

Only proceed with “valid” emails to protect sender reputation.[^2_9][^2_10]

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 3 – Infrastructure setup (critical, one‑time cost)

**Do NOT use your primary domain.** Set up 2–3 lookalike domains (e.g., `getridwan.dev`, `ridwanlabs.com`).[^2_4][^2_3]

**Cheapest reliable setup:**

- **Domains**: NameSilo or Cloudflare (~\$9/year each).[^2_11][^2_5]
- **Email**:
    - Option A (free): Use your existing Gmail + “Send mail as” aliases (riskier at scale).
    - Option B (recommended): 2–4 Google Workspace inboxes via a reseller (~\$2.50–\$3/inbox/month) or standard GWS (\$6/inbox).[^2_12][^2_4]
- **DNS**: Configure SPF, DKIM, DMARC (p=none initially).[^2_5][^2_3]
- **Warmup**: 2–4 weeks, starting 5–10 emails/day/inbox, ramp to 30–50/day.[^2_3][^2_9]

**Total infra cost:** ~\$20–\$40 for domains + ~\$10–\$24/month for 2–4 inboxes.

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 4 – Multi‑touch sequence automation (the “advanced” part)

**Goal:** Run a 5–7 touch sequence across email + LinkedIn + X, stopping on reply.

**Sequence structure (14–21 days):**

1. **Day 0**: Email \#1 (short, personalized, 1 CTA)
2. **Day 2**: LinkedIn connection request (note referencing email)
3. **Day 3**: Email \#2 (value: 1 quick audit insight)
4. **Day 6**: X/Twitter reply or DM if they’re active
5. **Day 10**: Email \#3 (case study or teardown link)
6. **Day 14**: Breakup email (“should I close your file?”)
7. **Day 18**: Optional final value drop (Loom audit)

**Automation in n8n:**

- **Trigger**: New row in Google Sheets with `status = new_lead`.
- **Sub‑workflows**:
    - Send Email \#1 via Gmail module → update `last_touch`, `next_touch_date`, `touch_count`.
    - Scheduler node: run every hour, filter rows where `next_touch_date <= now` and `status != replied`.
    - Conditional branches: if `touch_count = 1` → send Email \#2; if `touch_count = 2` → send LinkedIn request (via Make + PhantomBuster/Dripify alternative or manual queue).
    - **Reply detection**: Gmail “Watch Emails” node → if reply from lead → set `status = replied` and stop sequence.[^2_13][^2_9]

**Make.com role:**

- Handle LinkedIn/X actions if n8n plugins are limited (e.g., use Make’s LinkedIn + Twitter modules).
- Sync all activity back to Google Sheets for a single source of truth.[^2_14][^2_1]

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 5 – Reply triage \& booking (semi‑automated)

- When a lead replies, n8n/Gmail node tags the row as `replied`.
- Make scenario: if `status = replied` → send you a Slack/Telegram alert with lead info.
- You manually reply within 24 hours (automation stops here to keep it human).[^2_9]
- Use a free Calendly link in your signature or Email \#2 to reduce back‑and‑forth.

______________________________________________________________________

## Stage 6 – Measurement \& iteration

Track in Google Sheets:

- `sent_count`, `reply_count`, `meeting_booked`, `client_won`.
- Weekly: calculate reply rate (target 5–15%) and iterate subject lines, hooks, and niches.[^2_15]

A/B test:

- Different first lines (funding vs. UX issue vs. SEO gap).
- Different CTAs (“15‑min audit” vs. “free teardown video”).

**Google Sheet columns:**
`brand | url | niche | signal | contact_name | email | email_status | status | last_touch | next_touch_date | touch_count | notes`

**n8n workflow (simplified):**

1. **Google Sheets → Search Rows** (filter: `status = new_lead` AND `email_status = valid`)
2. **Gmail → Send Email** (template \#1, personalize with `contact_name`, `brand`, `signal`)
3. **Google Sheets → Update Row** (`touch_count +1`, `next_touch_date = now + 2d`, `last_touch = now`)
4. **Wait/Scheduler** → repeat for touches \#2–\#7 with conditional delays
5. **Gmail → Watch Emails** → if reply from lead → `status = replied`, stop sequence

**Make scenario (optional add‑on):**

- **Trigger**: Google Sheets row where `touch_count = 2` AND `status = new_lead`
- **Action**: LinkedIn → Send connection request with note
- **Action**: Google Sheets → Update `linkedin_sent = yes`

## 3) Cold email structure \& sequence (non‑robotic, high‑reply)

You asked to follow the method used by platforms like **Smartlead, Instantly, etc.**\
Here’s the distilled 2026 best‑practice framework they all converge on.[^5_19][^5_20][^5_21][^5_22][^5_23]

### Core principles

- **Under 100 words** (ideally 60–80).[^5_22][^5_19]
- **One problem, one proof point, one ask**.[^5_20][^5_21]
- **First line 100% about them**, not you.[^5_24][^5_23]
- **Plain text**, no heavy HTML, no images/logos in first email.[^5_17][^5_16]
- **No links in Email \#1** (improves deliverability).[^5_16][^5_17]
- **Personalization that passes the “so what?” test** – must tie to a real signal.[^5_24]

______________________________________________________________________

### 5‑email sequence structure (days 1, 4, 8, 13, 18–20)

This mirrors what Smartlead/Instantly‑style playbooks recommend.[^5_21][^5_20]

#### Email 1 – “The Problem Opener” (Day 1)

**Goal:** Earn a reply, not sell hard.

**Structure:**

- Hook: specific observation/signal
- Problem: what this likely costs them
- Proof: 1 line of credibility (similar brand, metric)
- Soft CTA: low‑friction question

**Template (for intent lead, CRO play):**

> Subject: Quick note about {brand}’s checkout
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Noticed {brand}’s PDPs take ~{load_time}s on mobile and the add‑to‑cart button sits below the fold on some products.
>
> For stores like yours (e.g., {similar_brand}), fixing that alone lifted conversion 12–18% in 3 weeks.
>
> Worth a 15‑minute chat to see if the same wins exist for {brand}?
>
> – {your_name}

Key: no “I’m {name} from {company}” intro; start with them.

______________________________________________________________________

#### Email 2 – “The Value Add” (Day 4)

**Goal:** Add value, not just “following up.”

**Structure:**

- Reference thread
- One new insight (mini‑audit style)
- Same soft CTA

> Subject: Re: Quick note about {brand}’s checkout
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Quick extra: your collection pages load 2–3× slower than top DTC brands in {niche}, mostly from unoptimized images and unused apps.
>
> A lightweight theme tweak + image compression could shave 1–2s off load time, which usually means more add‑to‑carts.
>
> Still worth a quick chat?
>
> – {your_name}

______________________________________________________________________

#### Email 3 – “Social Proof” (Day 8)

**Goal:** Build credibility with a comparable result.

> Subject: A quick case study
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Recently helped {similar_brand} (same niche, ~{their_traffic} monthly visitors) go from {old_conv}% to {new_conv}% conversion in 4 weeks by:
>
> - Redesigning PDP layout
> - Speed optimization (Core Web Vitals)
> - Simplifying checkout steps
>
> If {brand} is happy with current numbers, no need to change anything. If not, I can outline the exact playbook.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> – {your_name}

______________________________________________________________________

#### Email 4 – “New Angle” (Day 13)

**Goal:** Approach from a different pain/trend.

> Subject: One more angle
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Another thing I see in {niche}: brands running heavy Meta/TikTok ads but losing 40–60% of mobile visitors before the page fully loads.
>
> You’re running ads for {brand} (saw your creatives), so even a 1s speed gain usually pays for itself quickly.
>
> Open to a short audit showing the biggest speed/CRO gaps?
>
> – {your_name}

______________________________________________________________________

#### Email 5 – “The Breakup” (Day 18–20)

**Goal:** Close the loop, prompt a final decision.

> Subject: Should I close this file?
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Haven’t heard back, so I’m guessing CRO/Speed isn’t a priority right now for {brand}.
>
> I’ll stop pinging you. If you ever want a no‑BS audit or a second pair of eyes on your Shopify setup, just reply and I’ll send it over.
>
> – {your_name}

5. **Scheduled scenario (hourly)**:
    - Filter rows where `next_touch_date <= now` AND `status != replied`.

    - Increment `touch_count`, set new `next_touch_date`.
6. **Gmail – Watch emails**:
    - If reply from a lead → set `status = replied`, stop sequence.
