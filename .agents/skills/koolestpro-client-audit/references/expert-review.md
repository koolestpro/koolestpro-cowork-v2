# Expert Review Mode

For well-optimized brands with in-house CRO teams, designers, and developers actively working on the store.

**What Ridwan is selling here is different.** It's not "here's what's broken and how to fix it." It's "here's what your team has normalized, what you're missing from the outside, and what experiments are worth prioritizing next." The value is external perspective, no internal blind spots, and strategic depth beyond what an in-house team can see about their own product.

**Tone throughout:** Peer-to-peer. Talk to the team, not just the owner. Don't explain what a CTA is. Go straight to the insight.

## SCOPE DETERMINATION

Expert reviews are almost always scoped — not full-store audits. After reading the brief, confirm the scope:

**Common expert review scopes:**
- "Review our new PDP design before we ship"
- "Second opinion on our checkout redesign"
- "Audit our advertorial and landing page funnel"
- "Give us an ICE-scored experiment backlog for Q[X]"
- "We're stuck at X% conversion — fresh eyes on why"
- "Review our above-the-fold A/B test variants"
- "Audit our post-purchase upsell sequence"
- "Tell us what our in-house team isn't seeing"

If scope isn't clear: *"What's the one specific thing you want me to focus on? I'll go deeper on that than a broad audit would allow."*

Never run a full 10-step audit on an expert client. It's the wrong product and signals you don't understand the engagement.

## EXPERT REVIEW FLOW

### PHASE 1 — UNDERSTAND THE CURRENT STATE

1. **Fetch the scoped area** (same site access hierarchy as standard audit)
2. **Read any experiments shared** — understand what they've tested and what they concluded
3. **Map what exists** — don't assume, look at the actual current state of the scoped area
4. **Fetch competitor equivalents** — how do 2-3 top competitors handle the same area?

### PHASE 2 — BLIND SPOT AUDIT

This is the core value of an expert review. In-house teams develop blind spots because they're too close to the product. Look for:

**Normalization blind spots**
Things the team has stopped seeing because they've always been there:
- Microcopy that made sense internally but confuses external visitors
- Navigation patterns that reflect the company's internal structure, not customer mental models
- Trust signals that exist but are placed where no one sees them (buried in footer, below fold)
- Value proposition language that's product-feature-led because the team thinks in features
- Urgency signals that have been "on" so long customers have learned to ignore them

**Optimization ceiling blind spots**
The team is optimizing elements that can't move the needle anymore:
- Testing button colors on a page with a weak value proposition
- A/B testing headlines when the page structure is the real problem
- Optimizing the CTA when the real issue is the offer isn't compelling enough
- Improving the PDP when paid traffic is landing on the wrong page entirely

**Recency blind spots**
Changes made recently that introduced new problems:
- A redesign that improved aesthetics but hurt conversion
- A new app that adds social proof but slows the page
- A "simplified" checkout that removed helpful friction signals

**Category blind spots**
Things common in other eCommerce categories that haven't crossed over to theirs yet:
- Advertorial funnels used widely in supplements/beauty but not their category
- Quiz funnels that work for skincare but the team hasn't tried for their product
- Bundle mechanics that are standard in complementary product categories

### PHASE 3 — EXPERIMENT PRIORITIZATION (ICE SCORING)

For expert brands, this is often more valuable than audit findings. They can run tests — give them a prioritized backlog.

For each proposed experiment, generate an ICE score:

| Dimension | Score | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| **Impact** | 1-10 | Could this meaningfully move the primary metric? (10 = could double it) |
| **Confidence** | 1-10 | How strong is the evidence base? (10 = customer research + competitor proof) |
| **Ease** | 1-10 | How hard to implement? (10 = copy change, 1 = full rebuild) |
| **ICE Score** | avg | (Impact + Confidence + Ease) ÷ 3 |

**Hypothesis format for each experiment:**
```
EXPERIMENT [N]                              ICE: [X.X]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Hypothesis:
"If we [specific change], then [primary metric] will
[improve/increase] because [reason grounded in evidence]."

Evidence base:
[What research, data, or competitor proof backs this]

What to test:
Control: [current state]
Variant: [specific change]

Primary metric: [what determines the winner]
Guardrail metrics: [what must not decrease]
Estimated sample needed: [rough estimate based on traffic]

Why this over other tests:
[Why this is the right experiment at this stage]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

Generate 5-8 experiments, ranked by ICE score. Mark which ones can run in parallel vs. which need to be sequential.

**Important:** Do not suggest "meek tweaks" (button color, font size, image swap). These rarely reach statistical significance and waste testing bandwidth. Suggest bold changes that could move the metric by 10%+.

### PHASE 4 — STRATEGIC GAPS (TIER 2/3 ONLY)

Skip Tier 1 basics unless you find something the team genuinely missed. Focus on:

**Tier 2 — structural opportunities**
Page types or funnel stages that don't exist yet and would unlock new revenue. Reference the page type table in `references/audit-tiers.md`.

**Tier 3 — emerging leverage**
Where the category is heading and how to get ahead of it — AI search, new ad formats, quiz personalization, subscription mechanics, etc. Use `seo-geo-aeo` and `marketing-cro:contagious` frameworks.

## TONE GUIDE FOR EXPERT REVIEWS

**Write like this:**
- Peer-to-peer: "Your team's current approach to social proof is working — here's where it breaks down"
- Evidence-first: cite specific data, competitor examples, research
- Opinionated: make calls, don't hedge everything
- Respectful of what exists: acknowledge what's working before addressing what isn't

**Never write like this:**
- Tutorial language: "Social proof refers to..."
- Condescending basics: "Make sure your CTA is above the fold" (they know)
- Vague direction: "Consider improving your value proposition"
- Over-explained recommendations: they have a team to figure out implementation details

## PRE-CALL QUESTIONS FOR EXPERT BRANDS

Use these instead of the standard discovery questions when the client is a well-optimized brand:

**About their current state:**
- What's your current conversion rate and where is it stuck?
- What's your test velocity — how many experiments per month?
- Walk me through your last three A/B tests. What did you learn?
- What has your team already ruled out or decided against? Why?
- What does your CRO team think the problem is?

**About the specific engagement:**
- What specific area do you want me to focus on?
- Is there a decision you're trying to make that this review should inform?
- Are there constraints I should know about — tech, budget, timeline?
- Who will read this review — the CRO team, the exec team, or both?

**About the competitive landscape:**
- Who do you benchmark against?
- Who's doing something in your category that you admire, even if they're not a direct competitor?
- Where do you think your competitors are ahead of you?

**About success:**
- What would make this review worth the investment for you?
- Is there a specific hypothesis you want validated or challenged?
